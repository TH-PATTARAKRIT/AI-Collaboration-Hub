## 5. Detailed Findings — Council Challenge (CP-03)

### 5.1 The central finding: the mandate question cannot be answered READY

Track 05's actual challenge question is whether warehouse/location/company/tenant boundaries, multi-warehouse flow, route/replenishment, performance/resilience, and the Accounting interface are coherent for SaaS scale. The Council pass's controlling finding is that this question cannot currently be answered `READY`, because no SMEsPlus target-side SaaS/tenancy/company architecture exists anywhere in the evidence chain to evaluate — and every relevant document (`A10`, `A13`, `A14`, `IER-003` file 08) explicitly and repeatedly disclaims making that claim, by design, per `DR-002`'s own Hard Prohibitions. This is not read as a defect in the prior research; it is read as that research correctly staying inside its own boundary. But it means the mandate's central question has, structurally, not yet been attempted by anyone, Team A or Team B.

### 5.2 Team B design status: zero execution artifacts

Consistent with that reading, the Council pass checked directly for target-side Inventory design work and found none: `TEAM_B_DESIGN/GROUP_A_SALES_INVENTORY_PURCHASE` holds prompt, readiness, and supersession scaffolding only, and no `TEAM_B_DESIGN/INVENTORY_CORE_BACKBONE` directory exists at all in git history on any branch. Team A's reference research is, in the Council pass's own words, "deep and rigorous"; Team B's target design "has not started." This is offered as a factual state check, not a criticism of sequencing — but it is the concrete reason the mandate question above has no target architecture to be measured against yet.

### 5.3 The Cross-Gate SaaS Invariants finding

The single most consequential item in the Council pass is a canonical governance fact verified directly: a Boss-approved ruling on `origin/SMEsPlus` (commit `e16b29f3`, confirmed a true ancestor of canonical, dated 2026-08-30) establishes ten mandatory "Cross-Gate SaaS Invariants" — `SI-01` through `SI-10`, covering tenant context, company context, template immutability, canonical identity independent of account code, no cross-tenant access, and related properties — that must be evidenced at every Accounting COA closure gate from `G01` onward, with an explicit audit-veto rule: an applicable invariant with missing evidence forces that gate to `HOLD`, and a violation forces `FAIL/FROZEN`. The same canonical evidence index records `COA-G04S` (the dedicated SaaS COA Tenancy/Provisioning/Versioning/Upgrade Architecture gate) as `BOSS AUTHORIZED / NOT EXECUTED`, `COA-G07` (Multi-company & Dimension Proof) as `NOT STARTED`, and its own Governance Red Flags section states plainly: "Multi-company / cross-tenant proof = NOT EXECUTED." The Council pass then searched every branch for an Inventory-side equivalent of this gate sequence or invariant set and found nothing at all. Accounting has a rigorous, Boss-approved, ten-invariant framework governing exactly the class of question this mandate asks — and it has not yet executed even for Accounting. Inventory, now formally recognized as its own independent "Stock Truth Backbone" architecture track, has no analogous framework or gate register of its own. The Council pass characterizes this as a structural planning gap, not merely an unexecuted step: there is currently nothing named for Inventory's own tenancy architecture to be evidenced against.

### 5.4 Company/tenant access control: verified with two open residuals

Company/tenant row-level access control is well-evidenced at the ORM layer for the reference source: a full 16-model `ir.rule` company-scoping table is cited from `stock_security.xml` (`IER-003` file 08, `VERIFIED WITH CONDITIONS`). Two residuals stand open and unaudited even for the reference system itself: whether `sudo()`-elevated code paths bypass these rules anywhere in `stock_account`, `sale_stock`, `purchase_stock`, or `mrp`; and the separately-registered database-layer gap (`SAAS-03`) — no CHECK constraint or DB-level row-security enforces any of this independent of the ORM.

### 5.5 GRPA-H8: a scope boundary, not a proof

`GRPA-H8` (the two uncoordinated Thai "branch" concepts) was closed "as an Inventory architecture question" by deferring to an "approved baseline." The Council pass reads that phrase strictly as a scope boundary — Inventory correctly does not own or redefine the platform's multi-company/branch model — and cautions against reading "approved" as "proven": canonical `COA-G07` status is `NOT STARTED`, and the same canonical index states "Multi-company / cross-tenant proof = NOT EXECUTED." The recommendation carried to §9 is that `CORR-005`'s wording be reviewed before any Gate decision could rely on the more comfortable reading.

### 5.6 Cross-company transfer flow: never traced end-to-end

The only evidence found anywhere in the chain for cross-company or inter-branch stock transfer is a `location.usage` taxonomy entry naming `transit` as the inter-company/inter-warehouse counterpart location. No `A`-series or `CORR` file walks an actual two-sided inter-company transfer scenario, and a direct keyword search across the state/lifecycle and stock-truth deliverables for "inter-company"/"cross-company" returns no hits. The Council pass names this a newly-surfaced evidence gap in its own right.

### 5.7 Database-layer invariants: confirmed unenforced

Four core stock-truth invariants — no negative quantity, no over-receipt beyond ordered quantity, one row per quant bin, and state-domain validity — are confirmed not enforced at the database layer in the reference source, corroborated via two independent evidence paths (DB forensics and source reading). The Council pass frames this as a genuine SaaS-scale concurrency/resilience risk pattern that has not yet been evaluated against SMEsPlus's own, not-yet-designed, persistence layer.

### 5.8 Concurrency control: a positive correction, still reference-only

Row-locking for quant reservation was independently re-verified to exist in the reference source: `IDR-007` located a real `try_lock_for_update()` call at `stock_quant.py:1082`, correcting an earlier "not traced" framing from a prior round. ### 5.9 The Accounting-interface fork

## 6. Detailed Findings — Special Team Investigation (CP-04)

### 6.1 Method and scope

The Special Team investigation was conducted under the mirrored IESA mandate, independently of the parallel Council challenge, concentrating on the genuinely open surface inside Track 05's own boundary: the `DR-002` deliverables `A5`, `A6`, `A8`, `A9`, `A10`, and `A13`, cross-checked against `A2`'s database forensics and against the `CORR-006`/`CORR-007B` corrective rounds where they touch this track's territory (the dropship route; G-7). Items already `CLOSED_WITH_EVIDENCE` in the CP-01 prior-evidence index were not re-litigated. Where the checklist pointed at a claim this track could independently re-perform rather than merely read, it did — most notably re-hashing and re-reading `stock_valuation_report.py` itself, and independently re-running the `git ls-tree`/`git merge-base` canonical-absence check.

### 6.2 Warehouse and location: coherent, with one SaaS-provisioning complexity

The warehouse/location model is confirmed more coherent than the risk register's framing alone might suggest. `stock.warehouse` carries no aggregate on-hand/forecast fields at all — it is a configuration record whose `reception_steps`/`delivery_steps` selections actively regenerate the warehouse's `stock.rule`/`stock.route` graph on write. This has a direct SaaS consequence the Special Team names `SAAS-04`: any future tenant-provisioning or warehouse-templating tool cannot simply copy warehouse rows between tenants or between a template and a new customer database — it must re-run the generation logic, or it will produce a warehouse with the right configuration fields and the wrong (or absent) routing graph behind them. `stock.location.usage` is confirmed as a clean seven-value enumeration (`supplier, view, internal, customer, inventory, production, transit`) that already accounts for every physical/virtual location role this mandate's questions ask about.

### 6.3 Tenancy: real ORM enforcement, no DB-layer backstop

The Special Team independently re-extracted the actual conditions behind `N-A13-02`'s `VERIFIED WITH CONDITIONS` disposition — a gap left open by the prior CP-01 reconstruction, which had never pulled the specifics from `CORR-005`'s own text. Doing so confirms `IER-003` read `stock/security/ir.model.access.csv` and `stock/security/stock_security.xml` in full and found comprehensive, company-scoped `ir.rule` row-level enforcement across every Stock-Truth-bearing model in the core `stock` module — `stock.quant`, `stock.move`, `stock.move.line`, `stock.picking`, `stock.warehouse`, `stock.location`, and ten others. That is a real and meaningfully strong finding: application-layer tenant isolation in the reference source is not an assumption, it is a directly-read, comprehensive access-control layer. The condition is exactly the boundary this track's mandate exists to police: `ir.rule` is an ORM-layer mechanism, not a database-layer row-security policy or CHECK constraint, and `SAAS-03`'s separate DB-layer finding remains fully open and untouched by it. At single-company, on-premise scale, ORM-only enforcement is a defensible, ordinary design; at real multi-tenant SaaS scale, where write concurrency and the surface area for a bypassed or buggy ORM call both increase, the absence of any database-layer backstop is a materially different risk than the reference architecture was ever built to withstand. `company_id` is separately confirmed as the *only* organizational scope anywhere in core `stock.move` (`SAAS-01`, `A5 §3`) — a SaaS tenant boundary is not inherited "for free," it must be independently designed and DB-enforced.

### 6.4 Grouping inherits scope; it does not enforce it independently

No `procurement.group` model exists in the reference source (exhaustive grep, zero hits); cross-domain grouping instead runs through a `stock.reference` model, itself scoped only by whatever `company_id` the linking Sale/Purchase/MRP document already carries (`SAAS-08`, `A6 §4`). This reinforces the tenancy finding from a different angle: cross-domain grouping inherits scoping rather than independently enforcing it.

### 6.5 Database-layer invariants and a related migration risk

Four DB-layer invariants are confirmed unenforced anywhere in the reference system, corroborated by two independent evidence paths (DB schema forensics and source-code reading): over-fulfillment is never capped, stock quantities can go negative, `stock.quant` bin uniqueness has no unique index (`_merge_quants()` only cleans up after concurrent writes race), and `move.state` has no DB CHECK/ENUM. At real multi-tenant SaaS write concurrency, this is a materially different risk posture than one company on dedicated infrastructure, and no DB-layer design work to close this gap has yet been authorized — it sits behind `COA-G04S`/SaaS Architecture Freeze, which per the Account-domain state available to this session is itself `NOT CLOSED`, alongside `COA-G04` through `COA-G08` generally. Separately, `stock_move_line.move_id`'s foreign key is confirmed `ON DELETE SET NULL` — more permissive than the ORM's own cascade expectations — a genuine migration/data-integrity risk if any tooling ever touches the database directly.

### 6.6 The Accounting coupling fork

The reference source's own implementation has Inventory's own code (`_create_account_move()`) directly construct and post the `account.move` journal entry — Inventory does not stay in its lane, it writes Accounting's ledger. `A9 §4` registers this as a fork it surfaces but does not resolve: replicate the direct-write coupling, or design a neutral valuation-event-plus-Accounting-owned-posting-service boundary instead. No round in the evidence chain since `DR-002` — not `CORR-005`, not `IDR-007`, not `CORR-006`, not `CORR-007A`, not `CORR-007B` — has picked this question up and decided it, even though `CORR-007B` went on to do five rounds of deep functional-design work on the adjacent `N-A12-01` close question. The Special Team recommends this fork be named explicitly to whoever eventually owns the Team B Accounting-posting-service design, because a coupling this tight is exactly the kind of pattern that becomes very hard to unwind later if it is inherited by default rather than chosen deliberately.

### 6.7 Sale/Purchase asymmetry and the missing partial-fulfillment primitive

Sale and Purchase integrate with Inventory through structurally different mechanisms for a conceptually mirror-image handoff: Purchase creates `stock.move` directly and synchronously; Sale creates it indirectly through the `stock.rule` dispatch engine (`A6 §6`, `A8 §1-2`). A future unified SMEsPlus route/replenishment engine must consciously choose whether to preserve or collapse this asymmetry. Underneath that, no shared "partial fulfillment" primitive exists across Sales, Purchase, and Manufacturing — each represents partial completion differently: a header-level enum, an unclamped received quantity with no ceiling at all, and an explicit, separately-recorded backorder MO, respectively (`A8 §5`). A future design can choose to unify this — a genuine coherence improvement over the reference — but cannot inherit a pattern for doing so, because none exists in the source.

### 6.8 Route/replenishment: dispatch mapped, idempotency not proven

The `stock.rule` dispatch engine is well-evidenced and reasonably disciplined: a single `run()` method groups procurements by action and dispatches to `_run_pull`/`_run_push`/`_run_buy`/`_run_manufacture`, and both `_run_buy()` and `_run_manufacture()` search for an existing open document before creating a new one. The duplicate/retry safeguard picture is honestly mixed, however: every guard found is a quantity-remaining-based merge or no-op, not a unified idempotency-key mechanism — a property the Special Team labels `PARTIALLY SUPPORTED`, not proven safe under all concurrent-retry interleavings. The dropship route (`GRPA-M16`) sits in the same family: `CORR-006`'s full read of `stock_dropshipping/models/stock.py` closed the evidence-missing question correctly, but its own disposition — carried to design as a dropship flow requirement — is unchanged by anything this track found and should stay tracked as a live route-architecture design input.

### 6.9 G-7, independently re-performed

The Special Team specifically re-verified G-7 through direct re-performance rather than citation. The exact file `CORR-007B` hashed (`ACCOUNT/01 ACCOUNT/SOURCE CODE/02 OTHER/stock_account/report/stock_valuation_report.py`) was located, its SHA-256 independently computed (`ca84b7604ca7b59a0d867effbda9758196f5cd2dbaca64b39e8e00d7b588fd4c`) and found to match `CORR-007B`'s manifest exactly, and lines 140–144 read directly: `action_print_as_pdf(self): return` and `action_print_as_xlsx(self): return` — both unconditional, valueless returns. The system-integrity framing added here is about failure mode, not just feature completeness: this is the report that computes the Initial Balance / Ending Stock / Inventory Loss / Stock Variation reconciliation — the literal Accounting-x-Inventory reconciliation artifact this whole reopen program treats as its goal — and its export buttons fail silently: no error, no exception, nothing to signal the action did nothing. This remains, exactly as `CORR-007B` disposed it, a bounded and non-blocking code defect owned by Team A/code-review follow-up rather than a Gate-blocking finding, but the Special Team cautions it should not quietly disappear from tracking simply because it sits several layers deep inside `N-A12-01`, which is not this track's own item to close.

### 6.10 Cross-company transfer: named mechanism, no workflow evidence

Cross-company/inter-warehouse transfer is only thinly evidenced: `stock.location.usage`'s `transit` value is documented by its own help text as the inter-company/inter-warehouse counterpart, but no dedicated inter-company transfer workflow, invoicing linkage, or route-generation pattern was found examined anywhere in the evidence base this track reviewed. This is recorded as `UNKNOWN`, not assumed proven either way.

The reference source itself lacks several "textbook" Odoo models Team B might otherwise assume exist: no `stock.valuation.layer`, no `procurement.group`, no `uom.category`, no `product.packaging`. ## 7. Clean-Room Impact

Both passes were asked the same question independently — does this pass's evidence, or the target-design implications of the gaps it found, put any clean-room principle at risk — and both answered **no violation found or alleged**, while identifying complementary, forward-looking risks from different angles.

**No violation found.** The Council pass states explicitly that the evidence chain "consistently and correctly treats the Odoo reference source as 'reference/learning/benchmark evidence only'" across `A0`, `CORR-006`, `CORR-007A` closure, and `CORR-007B`'s own Clean-Room Compliance Review (file 12), and that A10's own governing rule — "source behavior never proves SMEsPlus SaaS isolation" — is honored in every downstream document checked rather than quietly relaxed under schedule pressure. As a concrete positive example, the reference file underlying G-7 is nowhere in SMEsPlus's own git history despite being cited by SHA-256 hash and exact line number across four independent files, meaning it was hashed and cited from a local, non-committed reference area rather than copied into the clean-room repository — correct posture under Clean-Room Principle 2 (No Copy/No Clone/No Reuse). The Special Team pass independently reaches the same conclusion from its own, separately-sourced evidence: every finding in its scope is drawn exclusively from direct citation of the reference source plus the one file it independently re-hashed and read (`stock_valuation_report.py`), and "no SMEsPlus target schema, API, ORM model, route/rule engine, or code was authored, proposed, drafted, or implied anywhere in this track's output — every finding is phrased as a fact about the reference system or as an open fork for Team B to decide, never as a recommendation of what SMEsPlus should build."

**Where forward vigilance is needed — Council's framing.** The Council pass identifies the risk as entirely prospective, concentrated at the boundary where `COA-G04S` and its (currently nonexistent) Inventory-side equivalent eventually execute. Team B must carry forward only the business-semantic requirement evidenced here — for example, "the business needs comprehensive company-scoped row-level access across roughly a dozen-plus stock-bearing models," or "warehouse configuration must be regenerated per tenant, not merely copied" — and must not carry forward the Odoo-specific *mechanism* used to satisfy that requirement: its particular `ir.rule`/record-rule XML framework, its ORM-level `sudo()` escape hatch, its specific `try_lock_for_update()` helper, or its category-vs-location account-field split, as if any of these were architecture to be ported. The Council pass's own recommendation to Boss is that any future `COA-G04S`/Inventory-tenancy execution session be given this instruction explicitly rather than left to infer it.

**Where forward vigilance is needed — Special Team's framing.** Two structural findings *reduce* forward clean-room risk in the Special Team's assessment: the reference itself lacks several models a from-memory assumption about "standard Odoo" would expect (no `stock.valuation.layer`, no `procurement.group`, no `uom.category`, no `product.packaging`), so there is objectively less to be tempted to copy — SMEsPlus must originate clean-room equivalents regardless. One finding *raises* clean-room vigilance and should be carried to Team B explicitly: Product Category is confirmed as both the valuation-policy owner and the routing/removal-strategy/putaway-policy owner in the reference source. A clean-room Category redesign must independently derive both roles as SMEsPlus business semantics on their own merits — the reference's choice to bundle "how do I value this" with "how do I put this away" onto one category record is a historical accretion of that specific codebase, not a proven domain law, and adopting the coupling unexamined "would be exactly the kind of unintentional architecture-copying the Clean-Room principles exist to prevent."

**Conclusion.** No clean-room violation is found or alleged by either pass. Both agree the material risk is upstream of copying — a default-by-absence or bundle-by-inheritance risk to Principle 3 (Migrate Business Facts + Business Semantics Only), not a Principle 1/2 breach — and both agree it requires an explicit, affirmative instruction to whichever future session executes Inventory's own tenancy and Category design, not merely the passage of time. This document carries both passes' specific do-not-inherit lists forward together (§7 above) as the concrete content of that instruction.
