## 1. Mandate and Method

Track 07 (Security / Privacy / Resilience VETO) does not evaluate the substantive correctness of Inventory's functional-design or accounting-interface conclusions — those belong to other tracks' mandates. Track 07's own mandate, as scoped by the Full Reopen Program and the 9 Veto Council Charter, is: **IAM and permission granularity for stock operations (receive/deliver/transfer/adjust/scrap/count); tenant, company, and warehouse isolation; destructive and fraud-prone action controls; privileged override; audit trail; concurrency and recovery; backup/recovery implications for Stock Truth; and interface authentication/authorization where architecture evidence exists.**

Two bodies executed this identical mandate in parallel, independently, against the same Inventory Core Backbone evidence chain — **DR-002 → IER-003 → CORR-005 → IDR-007 → CORR-006 → CORR-007A → CORR-007B** (nine execution branches, none merged to canonical `origin/SMEsPlus`):

- **Council (challenge)** — a first-pass challenge whose primary assigned task was to independently re-confirm, directly from CORR-005's own text and its full underlying chain, the specific conditions behind N-A13-02's `VERIFIED WITH CONDITIONS` disposition (company ACL / tenant isolation), since a prior reconstruction pass (CP-01) had extracted only the headline disposition and had explicitly flagged the conditions themselves as this Track's job to extract.
- **Special Team (investigation)** — a mirrored, separately-run investigation of the identical mandate, using prior Inventory evidence as a starting point per the Special Team Investigation Rule rather than re-deriving from a blank state, with the same N-A13-02 re-confirmation as its own primary assigned task.

Per the Charter's Anti-Groupthink Rule, neither body had access to the other's output while working. This document is the first point at which the two are read side by side. Both bodies read primary-source deliverable text directly — CORR-005's Five-High Reconciliation Matrix and Residual Blocker Register, IER-003 file 08, IDR-007 files 03 and 06, CORR-007B's functional-design and count-freeze proof files — rather than relying on a condensed summary layer, and both used direct git commands (`git merge-base --is-ancestor`, `git ls-tree`, `git grep`) where the underlying question was one of evidence currency or citation traceability.

### 2.2 Why these verdicts conflict, and why this document reconciles to `HOLD`

**Step 1 — is this a factual contradiction, or a difference in threshold judgment applied to the same facts?** Overwhelmingly the latter. Reading both bodies' full findings side by side, the two independently reached the *same* qualitative conclusions on nearly every substantive question both examined:

- N-A13-02's ORM-layer enforcement mechanism is real, comprehensive across all 16 Stock-Truth models, and independently re-confirmed — **both agree**, in near-identical language.
- SAAS-03 (no DB-layer CHECK/RLS backing the ORM rule) remains open and unchanged — **both agree**.
- The `sudo()`-bypass code-path audit across `stock_account`/`sale_stock`/`purchase_stock`/`mrp` has never been performed by any session — **both agree**.
- Operation-level role/CRUD segregation for adjust/scrap/count has never been itemized by any deliverable — **both agree**, and Special Team independently names this a new finding in its own right.
- PDPA/privacy research is entirely absent from this evidence chain — **both agree**, independently, via their own exhaustive keyword searches.
- Append-only move history (no un-confirm path on a done stock movement) is a genuine, well-evidenced positive control — **both agree**.
- The `stock_security.xml` line-count citation (108 lines per IER-003 vs. 169 lines / 30 records per IDR-007) does not reconcile — **both flag this**, independently, in nearly identical terms.

Where the two bodies' *conclusions* about the same fact genuinely diverge is narrower: on **N-CONC-01** (row-locking for concurrent `stock.quant` reservation), Council treats the evidence as still requiring a bounded verification pass before being treated as settled either way, while Special Team treats the same evidence as already showing the register was more conservative than warranted. This is the one place in the whole comparison where the two bodies assign a **materially different disposition to an identical, specifically-cited piece of evidence** — see §3.2 for the full side-by-side.

**Step 2 — does the verdict gap trace to something beyond that one item?** Yes: it traces mainly to a difference in what each body treats as sufficient grounds for a VETO track to withhold reliance versus continue with notes, not to a disagreement about what the evidence itself shows. Council's own text is explicit that its `HOLD` is a recommendation gating *reliance on this specific mandate area*, not a freeze on the reopen generally, and lists four bounded, named conditions (§7) rather than an open-ended blocker. Special Team's own text is equally explicit that none of the gaps it independently found — several of which (`G-2`'s unaudited kill-switch, the idempotency gap, backup-tooling fragility, the FK cascade choice, the Accounting-side A16 dependency) are net-new findings Council's parallel pass did not surface at all — individually or together rise to the bar it believes a Security/Privacy/Resilience VETO should apply before recommending `CONTINUE_WITH_NOTES` rather than `HOLD`. Both bodies are, in effect, applying the same evidentiary picture to a genuinely judgment-dependent question — *how much named-but-unclosed residual risk is tolerable in a VETO track before Boss's Gate decision* — and reaching different answers. Notably, Special Team's own list of items it explicitly wants carried forward before any Team B design freeze (sudo audit, role/CRUD granularity, `G-2`/`G-3` weighting, a conscious SAAS-03 design decision, completing `N-DB-01`, PDPA research, the A16 dependency) is nearly as long as Council's own `HOLD` conditions — the disagreement is real, but it is a disagreement about the *label*, sitting on top of a large area of substantive agreement about the *facts*.

**Step 3 — reconciliation.** Because a genuine verdict-label conflict exists — not merely an asymmetry of coverage — this document does not attempt to adjudicate which body's threshold is correct; that judgment belongs to Boss. Consistent with the operating instruction for this convergence effort, the reconciled Track 07 verdict is **`HOLD`**, the more conservative of the two submitted verdicts. This reconciliation should not be read as this document siding with Council's reasoning over Special Team's — both narratives are carried forward at full weight in §3, §6, and §7, and Special Team's `CONTINUE_WITH_NOTES` framing, including its explicit statement that nothing found rises to a disqualifying or fabrication-risk level, is preserved verbatim as one of the two inputs Boss is deciding between. What this reconciliation does assert is procedural: when a VETO track's two independent, blind reviewers do not converge on the same headline recommendation, the convergence document itself should not break that tie, and the safer default for an unresolved VETO-track disagreement is the more conservative label, pending Boss's own review of both full narratives and the bounded next steps named in §7.

## 3. Detailed Findings

### 3.1 N-A13-02 — the assigned primary task, independently reconfirmed by both bodies

This is the strongest point of convergence in the entire comparison, and it is worth stating first because it is the one place where the evidentiary chain is deepest: **four independent reads of the same primary source** — IER-003's own review, CORR-005's reconciliation, IDR-007's re-performance, and now both this Council pass and this Special Team pass — all reach the identical conclusion.

`stock/security/stock_security.xml` contains an `ir.rule` block, under a `<data noupdate="1">` marker with an explicit company-scoping comment, applying `domain_force` filters of the form `[('company_id', 'in', company_ids)]` (or the `+[False]` variant for globally-shared records) across sixteen models: `stock.picking`, `stock.picking.type`, `stock.putaway.rule`, `stock.lot`, `stock.warehouse`, `stock.location`, `stock.move`, `stock.move.line`, `stock.quant`, `stock.warehouse.orderpoint`, `stock.rule`, `stock.route`, `stock.package`, `stock.scrap`, `report.stock.quantity`, and `stock.storage.category` — every model this evidence chain's own A3–A9 identify as Stock-Truth-bearing, not a partial set. Both bodies independently traced this from the primary file itself (via `08_IER003_HIGH_H5_COMPANY_ACL_TENANT_REVIEW.md`, commit `45c749ea`), through CORR-005's Five-High Reconciliation Matrix and Residual Blocker Register, to IDR-007's own re-performance, and both land on the same disposition: the ORM-layer enforcement question is **answered and closed**; a narrower, explicitly-scoped residual is **carried forward**, not silently folded into closure.

That residual is precise and twofold, and both bodies extract it identically:

1. **SAAS-03** — no database-layer `CHECK` constraint, unique index, or Postgres row-level-security policy backs any of this enforcement. `ir.rule` is evaluated in Python at ORM query-build time; a direct-SQL client, or any `sudo()`-elevated code path, is structurally unaffected by it. Both bodies confirm this is tracked separately in A10's SaaS/Tenant/Company/Warehouse Boundary Risk Register and remains open and unchanged.
2. **The `sudo()`-bypass code-path audit** — whether every stock-mutating code path in `stock_account`, `sale_stock`, `purchase_stock`, and `mrp` actually routes through the standard ORM (and is therefore protected) versus calling `sudo()` internally (which bypasses `ir.rule` by design) has never been audited by any session in this chain. Both bodies confirm this explicitly, and Council additionally traces it to IDR-007's own Controlled Carry-Forward Audit, which independently examined this exact item and issued its own `PASS` confirming it as a properly owned, disclosed, non-blocking residual rather than a silently abandoned gap.

Two evidence-hygiene defects surfaced during this re-verification, neither of which overturns the substantive finding:

- **Council alone** ran `git merge-base --is-ancestor` and `git ls-tree` against CORR-005's and CORR-007B's own trees and found that IER-003 file 08 — the document every later citation (CORR-005, A13, A14) points to as N-A13-02's controlling evidence — does not physically exist in either tree; it lives only on the separate `audit/inventory-core-dr002-independent-review-003` branch (`45c749ea`), which is confirmed not an ancestor of either. The relative-path citations written into CORR-005's own documents are accordingly broken for anyone who checks out CORR-005 or CORR-007B alone. Special Team's own account is consistent with this (it also reached the file via commit hash rather than an in-tree relative path) but does not perform or report the same tree-ancestry forensics, so this specific defect is carried here as a Council-only finding — see §5, row 23.
- **Both bodies** flag, independently and in nearly identical terms, that IER-003 reports `stock_security.xml` as 108 lines containing the sixteen-row table, while IDR-007's independent re-read of the same file reports 169 lines and 30 total `<record>` elements, citing six example line numbers (75, 81, 87, 93, 99, 105) for the `company_id` rules. Both bodies agree the two readings are not necessarily contradictory — a 30-record file plausibly contains the 16 company-scoped rules plus other record types — but the raw counts do not reconcile and no session has explained the gap. Both recommend this for Track 01 or a short Team A follow-up rather than treating it as grounds to distrust the qualitative finding.

### 3.2 Concurrency and recovery — N-CONC-01 (the one genuine item-level conflict) and idempotency

`N-CONC-01` (DB-level row-locking for `stock.quant` reservation under concurrency) is the one item in this entire comparison where Council and Special Team assign **materially different dispositions to the identical, specifically-cited piece of evidence.**

| | Council's disposition | Special Team's disposition |
|---|---|---|
| Underlying fact | `try_lock_for_update()` call inside `_update_available_quantity`, `stock_quant.py:1082` — spotted by IDR-007 on 2026-09-01, never cited by DR-002, IER-003, or CORR-005 | Identical fact, identically sourced |
| Reading | Concurrency posture is **not yet characterized** — locking mode, coverage across every quant-mutation path, and sufficiency against the named race pattern were never verified by any of the two subsequent rounds (CORR-007A, CORR-007B) that had the opportunity to | Concurrency posture is **better than the register states** — the register's `EVIDENCE_MISSING` framing is more conservative than the primary source actually supports, though isolation level and exact scope were "not further characterized by any pass" |
| Label used | `UNKNOWN WITH AN UNFOLLOWED LEAD` | `PARTIALLY VERIFIED` |
| Treated as blocking? | Yes — one of four named conditions in Council's `HOLD` (§7) | No — not listed among Special Team's own open risks |

Both bodies cite the same source (IDR-007 file 06, Part C, item 1) and neither disputes the other's underlying fact; the disagreement is entirely in how much weight an unaudited-but-promising lead should carry. This item is classified `CONFLICTING` in §5 rather than folded into either side's framing.

Separately, and **found only by Special Team**: no unified idempotency-key mechanism exists anywhere in the confirm/reserve/done pipeline. Every duplicate/retry guard identified in this chain is a quantity-remaining computation, which the evidence register's own words describe as "not mathematically equivalent to a hard idempotency guarantee under all possible concurrent-retry interleavings" (A13 Part 3, corroborated by A4 §7). Under a crash-recovery or network-retry replay scenario, protection against a duplicate physical-movement effect is best-effort, not guaranteed. Council's parallel pass does not address idempotency at all — this is carried forward here at full weight as a Special-Team-only finding, not diminished for having a single source.

### 3.3 Destructive and fraud-prone stock actions; privileged override

Both bodies converge on the reference system's count-conflict mechanism (`N-A7-01`, fully traced in CORR-007B file 02 via `action_apply_inventory()` and both the `stock.inventory.conflict`/`stock.inventory.warning` wizards): conflict detection is soft only. An intervening stock move against a location under count is flagged (`is_outdated`) and routed to a transient wizard, but nothing blocks the intervening move, and neither resolution path ("keep counted quantity" or "keep difference") undoes or requires elevated approval for the movement that caused the conflict. Both bodies agree this is correctly left as a Team B design-freeze decision among four named options (hard freeze; adopt the soft-conflict default as-is; location/session-level lock; manager-exception workflow), not decided here. Council frames this as "the single most concrete destructive/fraud-prone-action-plus-privileged-override evidence item in the whole chain" and adds a named flag for whichever option Team B selects: adopting the bare soft-conflict default as-is means any user permitted to apply a count can silently absorb an unauthorized or erroneous interim movement into the adjustment quantity with no privileged sign-off step.

A second, closely related backdate/lock-period item is already tracked under `N-A12-01` (Accounting/Inventory-owned, not this Track's to close) but has direct Security/Resilience materiality both bodies flag explicitly:

- **`G-3`** — lock-date/backdate protection (`_check_backdate_allowed()`/`_is_date_in_lock_period()`) is evidenced only at the `stock.picking` header level; Inventory Adjustments post directly as `stock.move` records via `stock.quant._apply_inventory()`, a path that need not touch a picking at all. **Both bodies** confirm this from the same CORR-007B/CORR-006 evidence — a multi-line transfer is blocked or allowed as a whole, and adjustment-only moves may not inherit the same period-lock protection ordinary transfers get.
- **`G-2`** — found only by **Special Team**, and not mentioned anywhere in Council's independent pass: Inventory's only bypass for that backdate protection is a single global `ir.config_parameter` kill-switch (`skip_lock_date_check`), with no user scoping, reason, expiry, or state tracking. Special Team contrasts this directly against Accounting's own equivalent mechanism (`account.lock_exception`), read in full from the same CORR-007B file (08, §9–10, commit `0eb78c68`): per-user or global-with-blank-user, reasoned, optionally time-boxed, state-tracked, and explicitly excluding the hard lock date from any exception at all. Special Team characterizes the asymmetry plainly — Inventory's kill-switch disables backdate protection for every user and company sharing that parameter scope, with no record of who set it, why, or for how long. This is a genuinely material, evidenced privileged-override and audit-trail gap that Council's blind, independent pass simply did not surface. It is carried forward here at full weight, not discounted for having one source — consistent with how this same reopen's Track 01 convergence document treated its own single-sourced findings.

### 3.4 Permission granularity — tenant, warehouse, and operation-level

Three distinct axes of permission are in scope for this Track, and the evidence chain answers them very unevenly:

- **Company/tenant-level isolation** — answered and closed; see §3.1.
- **Warehouse-level (intra-company) authorization** — a multi-warehouse tenant where staff at one warehouse should not adjust stock at another. **Council** states this explicitly and directly: the full sixteen-row `ir.rule` table, read independently and in full by both IER-003 and IDR-007, scopes exclusively by `company_id`; no location- or warehouse-scoped row rule appears in either read. Special Team's parallel pass does not independently address this specific location/warehouse axis — its closest finding (below) is adjacent but distinct. This is carried forward as a Council-only finding.
- **Operation-level role segregation** (does a routine-transfer role differ from an adjustment/scrap role) — **both bodies** agree this is unknown. `ir.model.access.csv` — the group-based CRUD permission file — is confirmed present and read in full (78 lines) by both IER-003 and IDR-007, but neither publishes its actual group-to-model-to-CRUD content in any deliverable. Special Team names this explicitly as a new finding in its own right, independently confirming via its own grep for `group_stock`/`perm_unlink`/`perm_create`/role-based terms across the CORR-007B tree that no itemization exists anywhere. Whether scrap or manual adjustment requires a more-privileged group than ordinary receiving/delivery is currently **unknown**, not merely unconfirmed — both bodies use language to this effect.

### 3.5 Audit trail coverage

Both bodies agree the picture is genuinely mixed. On the positive side, both independently confirm (A4 §6, A7 §5) that done-move history is append-only across the entire reference source: there is no "un-confirm a done movement" action anywhere in the modules read, and the only path to correct a done move is a new Return or a new Inventory Adjustment. Both bodies treat this as a real, worth-carrying-forward resilience-favorable architectural pattern, not merely a historical curiosity — this is the strongest positive control finding in the whole Track.

Set against that, coverage is only partial. A7 §7 confirms a dedicated audit mechanism exists for manual valuation overrides (`value_manual`'s inverse writes a `product.value` record) and that `mail.thread`/`tracking=True` was observed on two `product.template` fields — but a full audit-trail sweep of the Stock-Truth-bearing models themselves (`stock.quant`, `stock.move`, `stock.picking`) has never been performed by any session, and neither body's own pass performed it either. Whether an ordinary count adjustment or a scrap carries a who/when/what-changed trail beyond the append-only move record itself remains an open question. Special Team's `G-2` finding (§3.3) compounds this directly: the one known bypass of backdate protection carries, by its own citation, no reason, expiry, or audit trail of its own.

### 3.6 Backup/recovery and referential integrity

This is an area where, read carelessly, the two bodies could appear to contradict each other; read precisely, they answer two different sub-questions and are consistent.

**Council's framing** is specifically about Stock-Truth data consistency around recovery: "what happens to on-hand quantities and reservations if the database needs point-in-time recovery mid-transaction." Council ran a cumulative keyword search across all nine branches and found zero hits for **Inventory-specific** "backup" or "audit_log" content, consistent with (and correctly deferential to) DR-002's own Hard Prohibition against researching SaaS/runtime/backup architecture. Council's conclusion: this specific question — restore-time reservation/quant consistency — has never been asked for Inventory, and no Boss ruling states whether it is Inventory's evidence burden at all.

**Special Team's framing**, found independently and not addressed by Council, is about backup/restore **tooling viability** rather than post-restore data consistency: the reference dump's PostgreSQL archive requires PG18-class client tooling and was independently found to fail restoration under PG16 tooling across three separate sessions (this pass's own attempt, GROUP A's original restore, and the Independent Evidence Review's fresh restore) — a real, reproduced, non-blocking-for-DR-002's-purposes operational fact (A2 §1–2). Special Team also independently found, via live database-schema forensics, that `stock_move_line.move_id`'s foreign key is `ON DELETE SET NULL` — more permissive than ORM cascade expectations, meaning a direct-SQL delete could orphan move-line records without any ORM-enforced semantics running — and a dormant-control caution: a reject-table pair (`purchase_order_level_reject`/`purchase_request_level_reject`) present in schema with zero rows in the live dataset, a reminder not to assume schema presence implies active enforcement. Special Team further confirms `N-DB-01` — empirical (not merely structural) testing of invariant-violation incidence against real data — remains blocked by sandbox/environment permission control, not resolved.

These are genuinely different, non-contradictory sub-questions: general backup-tooling compatibility (Special Team: evidenced, real, three-times-reproduced) versus Inventory-specific point-in-time-recovery data consistency (Council: never researched at all). Both are carried forward in §5 as separate rows rather than merged into one, precisely so a reader does not mistake one for having answered the other.

Special Team also names four specific Stock-Truth invariants with zero database-level backstop — negative quantity, delivered/received-over-ordered ceiling, one-row-per-bin uniqueness, and valid-state-only — dual-corroborated via both source-code reading and live DB-schema forensics, and calls these "the strongest, most consequential invariant-gap candidates in the entire register" (A13 Part 10). Council's own SAAS-03 finding names the same underlying gap at a more general level ("DB-layer enforcement of company isolation and stock-quantity integrity") without itemizing the four specific invariants. This is elaboration, not contradiction: both land on the same conclusion — no DB-layer backstop exists — at different levels of granularity.

### 3.7 Privacy / PDPA

The strongest second point of convergence in this comparison. **Both bodies**, independently, ran an exhaustive search across the full nine-branch chain and found **zero** mentions of PDPA, personal data, or privacy anywhere. Both agree this is not itself alarming — Inventory's core objects are product/quantity/location records rather than primarily personal-data records — but both also independently surface the same category of latent, never-evaluated touchpoint: an ownership/consignment field on `stock.quant` (`owner_id`), and delivery-address/contact data that necessarily flows from `res.partner` records into `stock.picking`/`stock.move` documents for named recipients. Neither body treats this as a defect in the reference system; both treat it as genuinely new, unresolved territory that should be explicitly scoped as a future research item — Special Team recommends this be joint with the Account/Legal tracks, before any production go-live decision.

### 3.8 Interface authentication and the SaaS Architecture Freeze boundary

Both bodies agree on substance: no SMEsPlus-side interface/API authentication or authorization architecture evidence exists anywhere in this chain, and none should be expected yet — this is explicitly out of DR-002's own scope (§13 Hard Prohibitions), correctly deferred to the unclosed SaaS Architecture Freeze / COA-G04S gate, and consistent with A10's explicit non-claims. Where the two bodies differ is in emphasis rather than fact: **Council** recommends Boss issue an explicit scope ruling on this boundary — comparable to the H3 ruling that explicitly kept Tenant/Company/Branch platform architecture out of Inventory's research scope — rather than leaving it as an implicit gap a future Gate reviewer might mistake for "already covered." **Special Team** treats the same absence as simply expected and does not press for a formal ruling in the same way. This difference in how much formal boundary-setting each body believes is warranted is itself one of the underlying inputs to the two verdicts diverging (§2.2).

Separately, and found only by **Special Team**: the one interface actually evidenced for cross-domain tenant isolation — the Accounting × Inventory data handoff (A16, scenario 9) — is only half-verified. Inventory's own side of company-scoping enforcement is now evidenced-ready via N-A13-02; Accounting's own side of the same shared-model enforcement is explicitly flagged in A16's own text as still pending. Special Team notes this is stated plainly in the source but easy to lose if only the headline "Inventory side ready" is read. This is a legitimate cross-track dependency for the eventual Accounting × Inventory Cross-Proof gate, not something this Track can close unilaterally.

Council notes explicitly that this is the first Security/Privacy/Resilience-mandated review of this evidence chain: a same-day, differently-composed "9 Veto Challenge Council" report committed directly onto the CORR-007B branch (a nine-seat, execution-delivery-role roster — Team A, Team B, Figma/UX, Team C, Team D, Leader Functional Design, Leadership Database Design, Lead Integration & Localization, Lead Code & UI Architect) applied its own challenge authority only to `N-A12-01` and never touched N-A13-02, SAAS-03, or any tenant/security topic. Special Team's own account does not make this exact "first pass" claim in these words, but is fully consistent with it: its own method description frames this Track's N-A13-02 re-confirmation as the one item "explicitly assigned to this track for independent re-confirmation," implying the same absence of prior security-lens coverage. This Track's findings are accordingly net-new, not a re-litigation of ground already covered from a security angle.

## 4. Clean-Room Impact Statement

**Clean-room impact: none identified by either body.**

Both bodies characterize their own method and findings identically at the substantive level: everything examined for this challenge/investigation is business-semantic and architectural fact-finding about the **reference** system (Odoo's `ir.rule`/`ir.model.access.csv` mechanism and the field-level behaviors around it), gathered explicitly for learning purposes under ADR-0005/ADR-0006, consistent with DR-002's own explicit non-claims (A10: source behavior "does not propose SaaS tenancy implementation, schema, API, ORM, or runtime isolation design" for SMEsPlus).
