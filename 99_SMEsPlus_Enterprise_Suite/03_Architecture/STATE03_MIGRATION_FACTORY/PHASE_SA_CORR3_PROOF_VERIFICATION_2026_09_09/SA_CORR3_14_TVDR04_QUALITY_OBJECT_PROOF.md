### 2.1 The pattern that produced the zero, and why it could not fire

CORR2 published its pattern in full, which is what makes this session possible. The `SA-D19` clause,
read from the same blob at §2.1:

```text
SA-D19  quality (control|check|hold|order|alert|plan|point|team|certificate|inspection|result)|
        nonconform|non.conform|\bNCR\b|incoming inspection|receiving inspection|goods inspection|
        inspection (outcome|result|plan|report|step)|
        reject(ed|ion)?\b.{0,30}(stock|goods|receipt|lot|material)
```

Every alternative in the object-name half requires **a literal space** between `quality` and the
noun. The corpus does not store object names that way. It stores them in the two forms every
machine-readable evidence artefact uses: **run-together capitalised identifiers** and
**separator-joined lower-case identifiers**. A space-separated English pattern cannot match either.

**`TV4-F-02` — the zero is a property of the pattern, not of the corpus.** The same CORR2 blob §2.2.1
records, in its own words, that *two reference-system object names were removed from the published
pattern* as a clean-room scrub, with the deltas measured. The scrub was correct as governance. Its
side effect was to remove from the instrument the only token forms in which the object is actually
written down in the evidence base. **A clean-room scrub of a search pattern silently converted a
positive into a negative, and the round that performed the scrub measured its effect on two other
patterns but not on this one.** `EVIDENCE-PROVEN` (§4.2 quantifies the exact blob set the scrubbed
pattern cannot reach).

This is a new instance of a known class — *a pattern that cannot fire yields silence
indistinguishable from absence* — with a new cause: **the clean-room control was the thing that
disabled the instrument.** Recorded as a method finding for the programme, not only for this item.

## 6. What a Quality object must carry — the business-fact requirement set

Derived from three sources, in this order of authority: (a) the Boss ruling's traceability and
routing clauses; (b) the flows the Inventory design already commits to; (c) the enumerated 76 / 47 /
24-attribute reference inventories, used **as a completeness checklist for questions to ask**, never
as a design to copy (see §10).

### 6.1 The inspection event — the minimum fact set

| # | Fact the object must carry | Why it is required | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| `TV4-R-01` | **What was inspected** — product, and lot/serial where the product is traceable | Ruling's traceability clause | §5 |
| `TV4-R-02` | **Against what** — the identity and version of the control definition applied, captured **as of the inspection**, not by live reference | Otherwise re-editing a control definition retrospectively rewrites what past inspections tested. The reference object holds a live reference only | §4.3 |
| `TV4-R-03` | **What the criterion was** — the acceptance rule in force: measured value plus tolerance bounds, or an attribute pass/fail rule | Reference inventory carries min/max tolerance and a measured value; SMEsPlus must carry the criterion, not only the outcome | §4.3 |
| `TV4-R-04` | **The measured result** and, separately, **the verdict** derived from it | A number and a judgement are two facts; conflating them makes a criterion change unauditable |
| `TV4-R-05` | **Quantities**, decomposed: presented, tested, accepted, rejected — and the identity that ties them to the movement quantity | The reference object carries tested / passed / failed as separate values; without them a partial acceptance is unrepresentable |
| `TV4-R-06` | **Which business fact occasioned it** — the receiving movement, the production order or operation, the delivery, or the return | Ruling's three routes |
| `TV4-R-07` | **Who** — inspector identity, distinct from the approver of any consequent disposition | Segregation of duties, consistent with the Inventory design's own control set |
| `TV4-R-08` | **When** — inspection instant, distinct from the accounting date of any consequent event | The programme has already recorded system-derived accounting dates as a defect class |
| `TV4-R-09` | **Company context**, and tenant context where the platform carries one — as a *guarantee*, not as a carriable field | §8 |
| `TV4-R-10` | **Evidence attachment** — measurement record, photograph or document, immutably referenced | Inventory's scrap flow already mandates evidence; inspection must not be weaker |
| `TV4-R-11` | **Immutability of the verdict, with reversal by a new referencing event** | Direct application of `BD-ACC-01`'s reversal semantics to a non-accounting fact, so that the two do not diverge |

### 6.2 The disposition — a separate fact from the inspection

**`TV4-R-12`.** A verdict and a disposition are **two decisions with two authorities and two times**.
The reference object fuses them: the inspection record carries a failure-destination location
attribute, so the routing of failed goods is a property of the *test*, not a decision taken
afterwards by someone entitled to take it. SMEsPlus must separate them. The disposition set the Boss
ruling implies is: **accept · accept-with-concession · hold · rework · reject-to-supplier · scrap ·
return-to-customer-owner**. Each carries: the deciding authority, the decision time, a reason from a
controlled list, and — where it moves goods or value — the identity of the resulting movement.

### 6.3 The non-conformance and the corrective action

**`TV4-R-13`.** A non-conformance is the *durable* record; an inspection is the *episodic* one. One
non-conformance may gather many inspections. The reference object already models this direction of
the link.
**`TV4-R-14`.** Corrective and preventive action must be an **object**, not two text fields: owner,
due date, verification of effectiveness, and closure authority. This is where the reference is
weakest and where SMEsPlus can be straightforwardly better (§11.6).

### 6.4 Supplier non-conformance

**`TV4-R-15`.** A supplier non-conformance must reference **the supplier and the commercial
document**, not only the goods. The reference object links to a counterparty, and the field-level
mapping records that attribute as **present in the source definition and absent from the deployed
persistence**. A supplier-quality record whose supplier link is not materialised cannot support
vendor performance measurement, a debit note, or a claim.

## 7. Cross-module consequence register

### 7.1 Inventory — tested against CORR2's own rule, and the rule holds

CORR2 established a rule worth reusing, and this session tested the quality-hold case against it
rather than assuming it.

> **Quality holds are a location state, not a product state:** goods awaiting or failing inspection
> sit in a quality-check or quarantine place and are therefore **visible in on-hand but excluded
> from available.**
> — blob `7eb849dfe2434e7153e206e0df30eb5ba69e7011` §7,
> `.../FINAL_SOLUTION/INVENTORY/V1_0/03_INVENTORY_FUNCTIONAL_DESIGN_V1.md`

composed with the governing valuation rule: a movement **between two internal places** changes
*where* stock is and creates no accounting consequence; a movement **crossing to a non-internal
counterpart** changes *whether the company owns* it and emits a valuation fact.

| Question | Answer | Basis |

## 9. Architecture alternatives, compared — and both are already in the corpus

The two candidates are **not invented for this artifact**. The corpus already asserts both, in two
`V1_0`/blueprint artefacts, and **no control has ever compared them**.

- **Alternative A — Quality as a state/disposition attribute of an inventory position.**
  Asserted by the Inventory functional design: *"Quality holds are a location state, not a product
  state."* Under A, quality is expressed by **where the goods are**; a hold is a placement, a
  disposition is a movement, and no new object is required.
- **Alternative B — Quality as a first-class bounded context with its own aggregates and
  lifecycle.** Asserted by both clean-room blueprints, which give Quality its own row in the domain
  map alongside Inventory and Manufacturing.

**`TV4-F-20` — SMEsPlus currently holds both positions simultaneously, in artefacts of comparable
standing, and no artefact reconciles them.** `EVIDENCE-PROVEN` by the two quotations in §4.1 and §7.1.

### 9.1 Comparison on stated criteria

Criteria are stated before the scoring, and are drawn from obligations already binding on the
programme, not chosen to favour an answer.

| # | Criterion | Where the criterion comes from | **A — location state** | **B — first-class object** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Satisfies the Boss ruling's six capability areas | §5 | **No.** A place cannot carry a plan, a non-conformance, a corrective action or a certificate | **Yes** |
| C2 | Satisfies the traceability clause | §5 | **Partially.** A place records that goods are held, not what was tested, against what, by whom, with what result | **Yes** |
| C3 | Supplies the *"inspection outcome recorded per line"* control the Inventory design already commits to | `TV4-F-12` | **No** | **Yes** |
| C4 | Availability semantics without a new mechanism | §7.1 | **Yes** — the strongest point for A | **Yes**, if B expresses holds by placement rather than by a parallel availability rule |
| C5 | Keeps ownership/valuation semantics unchanged | §7.1 | **Yes** | **Yes**, on the same condition as C4 |
| C6 | Supports supplier non-conformance and vendor claims | `TV4-R-15`, `TV4-F-17` | **No** | **Yes** |
| C7 | Supports the manufacturing gate | §7.4 | **Weak.** A place cannot express *"a mandatory test for this output is unsatisfied"* | **Yes** |
| C8 | Scope-aware declaration and platform invariant coverage | §8 | **Weak** — inherits the location's scope, which is not the inspection's scope | **Yes**, if each object declares its axis |
| C9 | Cost of implementation and of being wrong | judgement, stated as judgement | **Low cost, high cost of being wrong** — the missing facts are unreconstructable afterwards | **Higher cost, low cost of being wrong** — a hold can always be *expressed* as a placement later |
| C10 | Clean-room distance from the reference | §10 | **High** | **Moderate** — B's aggregate names collide with reference class names (`TV4-F-07`) |

### 9.2 Recommendation

**Adopt B, with A retained as B's *expression* of the hold — they are not exclusive, and treating
them as exclusive is what produced the contradiction.**

Precisely:

1. **The Quality object is first-class** — an inspection event, a control definition, a
   non-conformance, a corrective action, each with its own identity, lifecycle, scope axis and
   audit trail (`TV4-R-01`…`TV4-R-22`).
2. **The hold is still a placement.** A quality object does not introduce a second availability
   mechanism; it *causes* the movement that produces the hold. Availability continues to be derived
   from where the goods are, exactly as the Inventory design specifies. `TV4-D-01` survives intact.
3. **The disposition is a decision, then a movement** (`TV4-R-12`). The quality object records the
   decision and its authority; Inventory records the movement; valuation follows from whether the
   movement crosses to a non-internal counterpart. **No new valuation rule is introduced.**
4. **`RISK-U02` resolves as a consequence, not as a separate decision.** If the verdict and the
   disposition are recorded on a first-class object, a distinct damaged-goods *state* is not needed
   — a quarantine place plus a recorded verdict carries strictly more information than a state flag
   would. This is offered as a **design position that discharges the question**, and it remains
   subject to the Thai user validation `RISK-U02` requires. This session does **not** close
   `RISK-U02`; it supplies the design answer that validation should be run against, and leaves the
   register entry in Inventory's ownership where its status field puts it.

**Why B and not A, in one sentence.** Under A the facts the Boss ruling requires are not merely
unrecorded, they are **unrecordable**, and a hold that has been released leaves no trace that it ever
existed — which is precisely the *occurrence-not-status* failure the programme has already recorded
elsewhere.

## 11. Phase SA clean-room acceptance gate — the seven demonstrations

**11.1 Learned facts and semantics.** An inspection is an episodic assertion that a specified
population of goods was tested against a stated criterion at a stated time by a stated actor, and a
verdict was reached. A non-conformance is the durable record that gathers inspections and drives
corrective action. A hold is a consequence of a verdict, not the verdict. A disposition is a separate
decision with its own authority. The measured semantics of the evidenced object: 76 attributes on
the inspection record, 47 on the control definition, 24 on the non-conformance, 86 relationships to
31 tables, **0 of them financial** (§4.3, §7.2).

**11.2 What was NOT inherited.** §10, in full — seven named declines and one scope exclusion.

**11.3 Alternatives considered.** §9 — two alternatives, both already asserted in the corpus, scored
against ten criteria stated before scoring, with the recommendation showing they are composable
rather than exclusive.

**11.4 SMEsPlus rationale.** Every requirement in §6 traces to one of: a clause of the Boss ruling
(§5), a control the Inventory design already commits to (§7.1), an obligation `BD-ACC-01` places on
event identity and reversal (`TV4-R-11`), or a measured defect in the evidenced object
(`TV4-R-02`, `TV4-R-12`, `TV4-R-14`, `TV4-R-21`). None traces to "the reference does it this way".

**11.5 Tenant / Company / control / audit boundaries.** §8 — company-scoped and tenant-blind as

**11.6 What SMEsPlus does better or differently.** Five specific, testable differences: verdict

not a very-deep-research programme. Two proof gaps (`TV4-G-01`, `TV4-G-02`) are measurement tasks.
**`TVDR-04` should be reclassified from `Research` to `Design, with one bounded verification`.**
