| 17 | Concurrency | Two simultaneous actions on the same Entry silently produce a lost update | **New design principle (was GAP-D01-21, out of scope for Team A's read-only pass):** any state-changing action on an Entry must be evaluated against that Entry's current, latest committed state at the moment of the action. This is stated as a required property, not a specific mechanism (no implementation named). | The failed, stale-state action is itself logged | Re-submit the action against current state | The specific technique that guarantees this property is an implementation decision properly deferred past this domain-design phase |

| 19 | Prior-period error materiality misclassification *(new, added at CORR-B3-01/02)* | A Prior-Period Error is classified Immaterial when it is actually Material (understating the required retrospective restatement, IAS 8 para 42), or classified Material when actually Immaterial (over-triggering restatement machinery unnecessarily) | **Not a scenario this domain's design resolves by mechanism** — per CO-16 (B09, new), materiality is a policy/judgment input this domain never computes. | 20 | Delayed Fiscal Year Close declaration *(new, added at CORR-B4-03)* | A Fiscal Year's calendar End Date passes, but the authorized `FiscalYearClosed` declaration (CAP-09) is not recorded until days or weeks later (reconciliation/review taking real time) — risking Reported Retained Earnings silently omitting that year's earnings for the duration of the gap (`M-AUD-09`) | **Resolved this phase, by design, not merely mitigated:** Reported Retained Earnings inclusion is boundary-driven ("Elapsed" — the Fiscal Year's own End Date <= query date, B07 §1e), never gated on `FiscalYearClosed`'s declaration. A Fiscal Year elapsed-but-not-yet-closed is normal, expected operation — reports remain fully correct throughout the gap (B08 MP-12 Proof F), and the eventual declaration changes only posting-lock scope (CAP-04-extended), never any reported figure. Compared against two rejected alternatives (an explicit "Completed-but-Unclosed" reported-equity component; a mandatory atomic close before the next Fiscal Year may open) at [B13](B13_DESIGN_OPTION_TRADEOFF_REGISTER.md) DT-11 | The Elapsed/Closed distinction is itself the evidence — no separate flag or reconciling entry is needed to prove the gap was handled correctly, since nothing in the reporting formula ever referenced the declaration to begin with | None needed — resolved by design, not something a reviewer escalates or recovers from | Whether a real deployment's operational close process typically takes days or weeks was not independently measured by Team A (out of scope for a design-conceptual phase) — this scenario's resolution holds regardless of how long that gap actually is |
| 21 | Attempted post-reliance Fiscal Year boundary edit *(new, added at CORR-B5-05)* | An administrator attempts to change a Fiscal Year's Start/End boundary AFTER it has already governed a COMMITTED Entry, elapsed, or been referenced by an issued/consumed report — risking silent retroactive reclassification of which Fiscal Year transactions belong to, which years are Elapsed at any D, and what `ReportedEquity_Known(C,D,T)` reconstructs (`M-AUD-12`) | **Rejected as a silent in-place edit; routed through controlled semantics instead (B07 §1h, BINV-16):** the attempt is refused as an ordinary configuration update once reliance is detected, and instead requires an authorized, CO-15-tier `FiscalYearBoundaryChanged` action (B04, new) — a new versioned boundary, not an overwrite of the old one. A boundary change alone never moves an already-COMMITTED Entry's Fiscal-Year membership; a genuine reclassification requires a further, separately-gated action. Compared against a stricter alternative (permanent immutability after any use, B07 §1h Option A) and adopted for handling the pre-reliance case without an awkward workaround while preserving Option A's protection everywhere it actually matters | The refused attempt (if made through an ordinary configuration path) and the authorized `FiscalYearBoundaryChanged` event (if made through the correct path) are both independently logged, each with actor, timestamp, old version, and new version | Escalate to whoever holds CO-15-tier authorization for a formal, authorized boundary-change action; the Known view for any T before the change remains exactly reproducible regardless | The exact authorization tier for this action is a genuine open Boss-level policy question, not settled by this domain's own evidence — flagged as new Team B assumption #7 ([B15](B15_DESIGN_TRACEABILITY_MATRIX.md) §6) |

## Pattern Note

Six of the eighteen scenarios (6, 9, 11, 15, 17, 18) required this phase to go beyond
restating Team A's evidence, because Team A either correctly declined to analyze them
(concurrency, security/authorization depth — out of scope for a read-only forensic pass) or
found the reference system's own answer genuinely absent or unproven (future posting,
duplicate detection effectiveness, missing-reference traceability). Multi-tenant isolation
(6) was never in scope for Team A's evidence at all, since the reference system was not
evaluated as a multi-tenant SaaS platform. This is exactly the kind of gap Team B's clean-room
design mandate exists to close independently, not merely inherit as still-open.
