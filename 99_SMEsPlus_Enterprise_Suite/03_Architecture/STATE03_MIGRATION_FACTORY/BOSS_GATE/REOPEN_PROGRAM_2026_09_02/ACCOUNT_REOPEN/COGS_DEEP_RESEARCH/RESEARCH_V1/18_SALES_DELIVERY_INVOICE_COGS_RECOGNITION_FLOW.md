## 1. Purpose and Boundary

This file is the dedicated account-flow proof for the sales-side cost lifecycle required by governing prompt §18 item 19 and referenced throughout §8.2/§12:

`Sales Order confirmation -> Delivery validation -> Customer Invoice posting -> COGS recognition`

covered under both Periodic and Perpetual, across four timing patterns (delivery-before-invoice, invoice-before-delivery, partial delivery/backorder, same-period), and closing with an explicit statement of which single event the reference ERP treats as the COGS-recognition trigger in its default/documented configuration, and whether that is configurable. This file assumes file `13`'s Perpetual lifecycle and file `12`'s Periodic lifecycle as already-established background and does not repeat their full evidence base — it applies that evidence specifically to the sales-side sequencing questions the governing prompt calls out by name.

Foundational rule, restated: **Inventory emits facts; Accounting decides postings.** A Sales Order confirmation and a Delivery validation are Inventory/logistics-layer facts (quantity committed, quantity shipped). Whether either fact becomes a financial posting, and which account it hits, is an Accounting-layer decision under the reference ERP's own configuration — and, later, under `JT-04`'s eventual SMEsPlus decision, not under this file's authority.

## 4. Sales-Side Timing Patterns — Periodic

`Fact Status: VERIFIED` (`EV-18-02`: "Periodic: Post vendor bills as expenses by nature, and update stock valuation in the closing entry by reducing expenses (stock variation)").

Periodic's defining mechanic is purchase-side (vendor bill expensing) and closing-side (stock variation), not sale-side. On the sales side, Periodic's documented behavior is that **neither Delivery nor Customer Invoice posts a COGS/expense-account entry** — the Customer Invoice posts Income/Receivable only (revenue recognition), and the *cost* side of that sale is not separately recognized transaction-by-transaction at all. Cost recognition for the whole period's sales is instead folded into the **Stock Closing entry**, which derives a period cost figure from `Opening Inventory + Purchases - Closing Inventory` style stock-variation logic (file `12`'s dedicated subject; not re-derived here).

### 4.1 Delivery-before-Invoice (Periodic)
**REFERENCE OBSERVATION**: Delivery validates, decreasing Inventory-layer (stock report) quantity. No Accounting-layer entry posts. Customer Invoice later posts Income/Receivable only. Period-end closing derives the aggregate cost-of-sales figure.
**ACCOUNTING MEANING**: under Periodic, the *sequence* of delivery vs invoice has no accounting consequence by itself, because neither individually recognizes cost — cost recognition is deferred wholesale to period close regardless of when in the period delivery or invoicing happened.
**THAI RULE STATUS**: `HOLD`.
**SMEPLUS CANDIDATE / HOLD**: `HOLD`.

### 4.2 Invoice-before-Delivery (Periodic)
Same reasoning as §4.1 — sequence-independent, because Periodic does not tie cost recognition to either individual document.
**Fact Status**: `VERIFIED` for the general Periodic mechanic; `HOLD` for whether the reference ERP raises any warning, block, or exception-report line for invoice-before-delivery specifically (not independently traced in this pass; a plausible candidate is the "Invoiced Not Delivered" review line named in file `13` §5.6/`EV-18-01`, but that line is documented in the version-19+ Perpetual accrual-entries context, not confirmed as a Periodic-mode feature — routed to file `17`/`23`).

### 4.3 Partial Delivery / Backorder (Periodic)
`Fact Status: HOLD`. Not independently traced. The structural expectation carried from file `13` §7 row 5 (each stock-move layer is its own physical event) would still apply at the Inventory layer regardless of valuation method, but since Periodic does not post cost per movement, a partial delivery under Periodic has no incremental accounting consequence at delivery time in either case — only the *quantity* difference matters for the eventual period-end stock count that feeds the closing entry. This inference is `PROVISIONAL`, not `VERIFIED` against a worked backorder example.

### 4.4 Same-Period Delivery and Invoice (Periodic)
Immaterial distinction under Periodic by the same reasoning as §4.1 — same-period or cross-period timing of delivery vs invoice does not change *how much* cost is recognized under Periodic, only the period-end physical count does. `Fact Status: VERIFIED` (follows directly from the Periodic mechanic already quoted).

## 5. Sales-Side Timing Patterns — Perpetual, Pre-19 Regime

`Fact Status: PROVISIONAL` for the exact trigger (carried from file `13` §4.4's internal documentation tension), `VERIFIED` for the account roles involved.

Recap of the mechanism (file `13` §4.4, `EV-18-03`/`04`): Delivery posts a real-time journal entry decreasing Stock Valuation, with the counterpart in the **Stock Output Account** (interim, current-asset type under Anglo-Saxon). The documentation states delivering products *and* invoicing the customer jointly balance the Stock Output account. The Expense/COGS account itself is a separate account from Stock Output.

### 5.1 Delivery-before-Invoice (Pre-19 Perpetual)
**REFERENCE OBSERVATION**: Delivery posts immediately (Stock Valuation credited, Stock Output debited). Invoice posts later, at which point — per the account-role description, not an independently traced worked entry — the Stock Output interim balance is cleared against the true Expense/COGS account.
**ACCOUNTING MEANING**: this is the pattern the reference documentation is actually describing as the normal case ("delivering... and invoicing... balance the Stock Output account" implies delivery happens, then invoicing follows and closes the loop). The physical-asset-side entry precedes the P&L-expense-side entry by the gap between delivery and invoicing.
**THAI RULE STATUS**: `HOLD`.
**SMEPLUS CANDIDATE / HOLD**: `HOLD`.
**Fact Status: `PROVISIONAL`** — the *existence* of the two-step mechanism is well evidenced; the precise moment the Expense/COGS account itself receives its debit (at invoice-post instant, vs at some other reconciliation step) is not shown as a worked, dated journal-entry pair in the fetched documentation.

### 5.2 Invoice-before-Delivery (Pre-19 Perpetual)
`Fact Status: HOLD`. Not independently traced. If Stock Output is only created by a delivery move (per its definition as "counterpart... for all outgoing stock moves," `EV-18-03`), then an invoice posted before any delivery move exists would have no Stock Output balance yet to clear against — meaning either (a) the invoice posts Income/Receivable only and waits for delivery to trigger the stock-side entry, or (b) the reference ERP blocks/warns against invoicing undelivered goods, or (c) some other mechanism applies. None of these three candidates is confirmed by fetched evidence; this is flagged `HOLD` rather than guessed, per the Hard Rule against manufactured certainty.

### 5.3 Partial Delivery / Backorder (Pre-19 Perpetual)
`Fact Status: PROVISIONAL`. The "each new stock move layer generates a journal entry" mechanic (file `13` §4.2, `EV-18-03`) implies a partial delivery is its own stock-move layer and would post its own partial-value Stock Valuation/Stock Output entry, independent of whether the remaining backordered quantity has shipped yet. This is an inference from the general SVL mechanic, not a confirmed backorder-specific trace.

### 5.4 Same-Period Delivery and Invoice (Pre-19 Perpetual)
`Fact Status: VERIFIED` as a special case of §5.1 with a zero time-gap — both entries post within the same period, so no cross-period Stock Output balance survives to a closing report. No new evidence required beyond §5.1's mechanism.

## 6. Sales-Side Timing Patterns — Perpetual, Version-19+ Regime

`Fact Status: VERIFIED` — this is the strongest, least ambiguous evidence in this file, carried from file `13` §5.4.

Direct quotation: "Perpetual (at invoicing): Inventory valuation is updated when bills or invoices are posted. The expense account (COGS) is debited when invoices are posted." (`EV-18-01`). Confirmed independently by the responsibility table: Delivery = Inventory-only (`✓` Inventory / `/` Accounting); Customer Invoice = Accounting-only (`✓` Accounting / `/` Inventory) (`EV-18-02`).

### 6.1 Delivery-before-Invoice (Version-19+ Perpetual)
**REFERENCE OBSERVATION**: Delivery updates the Inventory-layer stock report only — no accounting journal entry posts. The goods sit as a physically-shipped-but-not-yet-accounting-recognized gap. Customer Invoice, whenever it posts, is the event that debits COGS and credits the Valuation/Stock account.
**ACCOUNTING MEANING**: the reference ERP explicitly accepts a physical/financial timing gap here by design — it is not treated as an error state. The gap is covered, if a period boundary falls inside it, by the closing process and by "Invoiced Not Delivered"/"Bill To Receive"-class accrual review lines (file `13` §5.6, `EV-18-01`) — though the specific line relevant to *this* gap direction (delivered-not-yet-invoiced) is most directly the accrual mechanism's revenue/expense-matching purpose described generally in `EV-18-01` ("Accrual entries are required when the delivery timing differs from invoicing").
**THAI RULE STATUS**: `HOLD`.
**SMEPLUS CANDIDATE / HOLD**: `HOLD` — direct `JT-04` evidence.
**Fact Status: `VERIFIED`.**

### 6.2 Invoice-before-Delivery (Version-19+ Perpetual)
`Fact Status: PROVISIONAL`. If Customer Invoice unconditionally debits COGS/credits the Valuation account regardless of whether a delivery has been validated yet (the quoted rule names invoice-posting as the trigger with no stated precondition of prior delivery), the literal reading of `EV-18-01` implies COGS could be recognized on invoice even ahead of physical shipment. Whether the reference UI in fact permits invoicing an unshipped sales order, or gates invoice creation on delivery status by default policy, was not independently confirmed in this pass — this is `PROVISIONAL`, not asserted as confirmed system behavior, because it would materially affect a matching-principle assessment (recognizing cost before the related goods have left the seller's control) and must not be guessed.

### 6.3 Partial Delivery / Backorder (Version-19+ Perpetual)
`Fact Status: HOLD`. Not independently traced. Because the version-19+ regime ties the accounting entry to the **invoice**, not to the stock move, the more material question is whether invoicing policy itself is "invoice on order," "invoice on delivered quantity," or "invoice on full order only" — this is a Sales/Invoicing-policy question (governing prompt Menu C — Product Accounting tab "Invoicing Policy or other accounting-impacting product settings," §6 Menu C) that this Perpetual-focused file does not resolve. ### 6.4 Same-Period Delivery and Invoice (Version-19+ Perpetual)
`Fact Status: VERIFIED` as the trivial case of §6.1 — no cross-period gap, so the closing/accrual mechanism has nothing to catch. COGS still recognizes strictly at invoice-post, per §6.1's rule; same-period timing only means the Inventory-layer delivery event and the Accounting-layer invoice event both fall inside one reporting period, not that delivery itself posted anything.

## 8. The Mandatory Single-Event Statement

Governing prompt file-18 instruction requires this file to "state explicitly, with evidence, which single event the reference ERP treats as the COGS-recognition trigger in its default/documented configuration, and whether this is configurable."

**Answer**: There is no single universal trigger across the reference ERP's own documented configurations — the trigger is itself a configured choice, and it has changed in kind across versions:

1. **Under Continental accounting with Periodic valuation** (documented as "the best practice in Europe," `EV-18-02`), no single sale-side document triggers COGS at all — cost is recognized in aggregate at the **Stock Closing entry**, a period-end event, not a transaction-level one.
2. **Under Anglo-Saxon accounting with Perpetual valuation, pre-19 versions**, the best-evidenced trigger is the **Customer Invoice** (clearing the Stock Output interim balance into the Expense/COGS account), with `PROVISIONAL` confidence on the exact instant, per §5.1.
3. **Under Anglo-Saxon accounting with Perpetual valuation, version-19+**, the trigger is unambiguously and explicitly the **Customer Invoice**: "The expense account (COGS) is debited when invoices are posted" (`EV-18-01`, `VERIFIED`).
4. **Delivery, in no documented configuration found in this pass, is itself the direct accounting-layer COGS trigger.** It is at most an Inventory-layer event (all regimes) or an interim-asset-side event (pre-19 Perpetual only) — never a direct Expense/COGS-account debit in the evidence gathered.

**Configurability**: Yes — explicitly and by design. The accounting standard (Continental vs Anglo-Saxon) and the valuation method (Periodic vs Perpetual) are both company-level (and category-overridable, per file `13` §3) configuration choices, not fixed system behavior: "companies may choose a different inventory valuation method based on their specific needs" (`EV-18-01`). The reference ERP therefore does not have one hard-coded COGS trigger; it has a configuration matrix, of which this file has evidenced three of its documented cells directly and flagged the remainder `HOLD`/`PROVISIONAL`.

**Fact Status: `VERIFIED`** for the existence and direction of configurability; **`VERIFIED`** for cell 3 (the version-19+ Anglo-Saxon Perpetual trigger, the least ambiguous finding in this entire file); **`PROVISIONAL`** for cell 2 (pre-19); **`VERIFIED`** for cell 1 stated at the "no per-document trigger" level of granularity (the closing-entry mechanics themselves are file `12`'s detailed subject, not re-derived here).
