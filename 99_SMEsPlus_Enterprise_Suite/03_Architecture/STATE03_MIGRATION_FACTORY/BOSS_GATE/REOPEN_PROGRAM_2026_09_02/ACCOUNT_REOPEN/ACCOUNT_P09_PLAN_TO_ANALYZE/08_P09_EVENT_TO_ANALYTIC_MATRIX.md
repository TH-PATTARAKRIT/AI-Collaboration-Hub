## 3. WHAT THE MATRIX SHOWS

### 3.0 A management record can exist and still assert nothing

Row E19 is the sharpest case found anywhere in this session, and it was **found by another process and verified here after publication**. A depreciation posting creates management records from the asset's own allocation — and because the allocation is copied to **both** legs of a balanced pair, and the analytic amount is the negated signed balance, the two records cancel. **The cost centre is debited and credited in the same posting.**

**EA-06.** An allocation shall be applied to the rows of an accounting event **that carry the economic effect being attributed**, never to every row of a balanced pair. Attribution applied symmetrically to both legs is arithmetically guaranteed to attribute nothing.

**EA-07.** Where an accounting event's rows are allocated independently rather than as one event, the system shall verify that the resulting management records sum to the event's intended attribution. Row E20 shows the complementary failure: independent per-row allocation of a balanced pair can produce a **non-zero residue** that corresponds to no economic fact.

### 3.1 Eight of nineteen events produce management truth with no financial counterpart
E4, E5, E7, E9, E10, E11 create costed management records with no ledger entry; E8 has a ledger entry and discards the link; E17 and E18 do the reverse — they produce ledger entries with **no** management representation.

**The two failure directions are both present, and they are not symmetrical failures of the same mechanism — they are two different mechanisms, each complete in itself.**

### 3.2 One business fact can produce three management records
E5: a single change to a work-order duration produces a work-centre-attributed record, an employee-attributed record and a project-attributed record, from three different modules, from the same hours and the same rate (EV-P09-111). None of them is marked as a different view of the same fact.

**EA-01.** The constitutional rule *one business fact → one financial effect* requires a companion rule: **one business fact → one management fact, with declared perspectives.** Multiple perspectives on one measurement shall be one record with several attributions, never several records carrying the same amount.

### 3.3 The allocation-to-ledger mechanism destroys its own trace
E17 selects amounts *by analytic account* and writes entries that carry **no analytic dimension at all**; the causing axis survives only inside a human-readable description string (EV-P09-047).

**EA-02.** Any mechanism that converts a management allocation into a financial effect shall carry the causing dimension **as data** on the resulting entry, and shall carry a reference to the rule and the run that produced it.

### 3.4 Conditional inheritance is unmarked
E12 inherits the allocation only under a condition on the tax record. The resulting entry therefore has rows that are allocated and rows that are not, with no marker distinguishing "deliberately unallocated" from "not yet allocated".

**EA-03.** Deliberate non-allocation shall be recorded as an explicit value, never as an absence.

### 3.5 Asymmetric inheritance is unmarked
E13 applies the allocation to one of two generated rows. The asymmetry is in the source and uncommented.

**EA-04.** Where a generated pair of rows is allocated asymmetrically, the asymmetry shall be a declared rule with a stated reason, testable in isolation.

## 4. THE TRIGGER-POINT PROBLEM

| Trigger class | Events | Property |
|---|---|---|
| **at posting** | E1, E12–E16, E19 | management record follows financial truth — correct |
| **at document state change** | E2, E6 | management record is destroyed by a document action — history loss |
| **on field change, no document event** | E3, E5, E7, E11 | management record changes with no business event at all |
| **on schedule** | E17 | recomputed daily until posted; the amounts change under the user |

**EA-05.** A management record shall be produced by an **event**, never by a field recomputation. The reference pattern's most-firing trigger — a duration recompute — is not a business event and cannot be audited as one.
