## 3. AAS+ Position on the Design Question

AAS+ **supports** the shape recommended in `05` §7 — a shared semantic kernel with separate domain engines — and attaches three conditions and one veto.

### Conditions

`AASP-C-01` — **The kernel's event identity must be defined before adoption, not during.** The adversary's strongest argument is that the four domains anchor differently: a contractual instalment, a period of an asset's life, a slice of an invoice line, and a point-in-time estimate. The proposed identity `(anchor reference, period start, period end, policy)` accommodates all four, but it has not been tested against a fifth domain or against inter-company recharge. Until the identity is specified and tested, "adopt a kernel" is a direction, not a design.

`AASP-C-02` — **Residue policy and day-count convention must be mandatory, un-defaulted policy slots.** The evidence shows four mutually inconsistent residue policies in one product, including one domain that *refuses* residue because its schedule is an external contractual document. A kernel that defaults these will manufacture the same incoherence with better ergonomics.

`AASP-C-03` — **The termination condition must remain domain-owned.** Depreciation terminates on value; deferral terminates on count. A kernel that unifies the loop will give one of them behaviour it must not have. This is `08` axis 9 and it is the one axis AAS+ regards as non-negotiable.

### Veto

`AASP-VETO-01` — **No implementation of any P10 mechanism may begin until the ruling on `P10-U-14` is taken.**

`P10-U-14` asks whether a posting constraint may alter a recognition period. Every mechanism in the reference product answers *yes, silently*. The worst reachable scenario in `15` §3 is a direct consequence, and it is invisible to every reconciliation an accounting team performs.

If SMEsPlus builds any recognition mechanism before this is ruled, it will either reproduce the defect or build a separation that the ledger design then contradicts. The veto is on **implementation start**, not on design work, and it lifts the moment the ruling is issued.

## 4. AAS+ Dissent From the Primary Author

AAS+ records three dissents, in the interest of preserving them rather than resolving them:

1. **The author's package leans on "no catch-up exists" more than the corrected evidence supports.** After `P10-R-02` the claim holds only for the validation path. Any downstream reader who takes the headline rather than the correction will carry a wrong fact. This is precisely the failure mode the programme's own standing rule was written for: **read a round's correction sections, never its summaries.**

2. **The author treated the reference product's separation as informative about semantics for most of the session.** It is informative about product history. `08` §1 now says so, but the framing shaped which questions were asked, and questions that were not asked are not visible in the answers.

3. **The author's enumeration was disproved, and the replacement is a floor rather than a total.** AAS+ notes that the package's design argument does not depend on the count — but the count appears in the argument's rhetoric, and rhetoric propagates. Every downstream use must say "at least".
