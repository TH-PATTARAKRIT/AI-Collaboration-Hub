### 2b. A1 business rules, gaps and CRQs against the source

- BR1 ("names unique case-insensitively"): **contradicted** (see C01). Names are unique case-sensitively. Free-text lookup is case-insensitive (C03), so the two paths apply different identity rules.
- BR4: verified as written. It is also broader in effect than stated, because the cookie defaults apply to every non-salesman internal user (O4).
- G5: partly settled statically (O3). A title rename changes the matching key, so links already carrying the old identifier resolve to a new auto-campaign. The effect on reporting remains a runtime and downstream question.
- G7 (concurrency): still open. The generator reads and then writes without locking, and the SQL constraint is the only backstop.
- CRQ-UTM-01: the premise needs correcting. Some collisions are suffixed and case variants are silently accepted.
- CRQ-UTM-09: confirmed and extended. Archived trackers are reused, and so are archived protected mediums (O5).

## 3. Semantic findings (business meaning: marketing attribution, multi-company SaaS)

- S1 **Two identity rules for one vocabulary.** Manual or programmatic creation treats names case-sensitively, while free-text resolution treats them case-insensitively and with pattern semantics. Attribution for "Newsletter" and "newsletter" can therefore split across two records, and which record a free-text value maps to depends on the model's ordering. This goes beyond A1's "X" vs "X [2]" example.
- S2 **Attribution is ambient, not event-bound.** Cookies are captured on every request for 31 days and are not extended by repeat visits. They are applied to any attributable record created by a non-salesman user of the browser. A backend user who clicked a tagged link can mis-attribute unrelated records they create manually. For SaaS marketing reporting, first-touch and last-touch semantics are implicit and cannot be configured (CRQ-UTM-06 stands).
- S3 **The vocabulary is tenant-global and editable.** There is no company field, and internal users hold write rights without delete. Any internal user of any company can rename a shared campaign, medium or source, which rewrites how every company's historical records are labelled. The GMVQ lens topics on company-scoped attribution and shared-value edits are directly engaged (C13, CRQ-UTM-02).
- S4 **Protection means "cannot delete", not "stable".** Protected mediums can be archived or renamed. Archived ones are still silently matched by free text (C03). The protection therefore guards referential existence, not the meaning of the vocabulary.
